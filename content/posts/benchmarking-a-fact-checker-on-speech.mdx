FACTUAL is a real-time fact-checking pipeline: Whisper transcribes speech, an LLM extracts the check-worthy claims, and retrieval-augmented verification judges them against live web evidence. The system is described on the [project page](/projects/factual). This post is about the part that is easy to skip and easy to get wrong: how to measure it.

## Why we built our own benchmark

Existing fact-checking corpora did not fit two things we cared about. They start from clean text, so they never test the speech recognition step. And they cannot tell you whether a model checked a claim or simply remembered it.

So we built 400 claims in two halves:

- **200 from the PolitiFact corpus**, published between 2008 and 2022, which may well be inside GPT-4o's training data.
- **200 scraped from PolitiFact.com after the model's knowledge cutoff** (November 2023), where the model cannot rely on memory.

Comparing the halves gives a clean read on retrieval versus memorization. Then every claim was rewritten with speech-like disfluencies, fillers and hedges and converted to audio, so the benchmark exercises the whole pipeline from sound to verdict.

## What the numbers said

We compared escalating strategies: zero-shot prompting, scratchpad reasoning, retrieval-augmented generation, and a self-critique loop in which a separate judge model checks whether the answer is supported by the retrieved evidence and sends feedback back into query generation, for up to 10 rounds.

On the post-cutoff claims, GPT-4o with the agentic setup reached 84% accuracy and 0.83 F1, against 63% accuracy and 0.58 F1 for zero-shot on the same claims. Gemini showed the same pattern, from 68% zero-shot to 81% agentic accuracy, which suggests the gain comes from the retrieval and critique design rather than one model.

## The weak spot

Every configuration struggled to say "insufficient information". Models strongly preferred guessing support or refute over admitting the evidence was not enough, and accuracy on that one label sat near 0% for the best configurations. A fact-checker that cannot say "I don't know" has a real limitation.

## Limits

The benchmark is small, political and English-only, so generalization to other domains and languages is untested. And verification still takes several LLM calls per claim, which caps how real-time the system can be without work on streaming recognition and lighter claim detectors.
